The best salon software for a beauty business in Ghana should give clients an easy online booking page, take deposits and payments by Mobile Money as well as cards and cash, send reminders on WhatsApp, track every stylist's commission, keep client histories and loyalty in one place, work smoothly on a phone, and be priced in cedis with a free trial. Many international tools were built for salons in Europe or America and miss several of these, so check each one before you commit.

## Start with the problems you want to fix

Before you compare products, write down what takes the most time or costs the most money today. For many salons the list looks like this:

- Bookings arrive by phone, WhatsApp and Instagram, and double bookings happen
- Clients forget appointments or do not show up
- Month end commission takes hours and causes arguments
- Nobody knows exactly how much stock is left until it runs out
- There is no record of who the regular clients are or when they last came

Rank these by how much they hurt. The right software solves your top two or three problems simply. A long feature list you never use is not worth paying for.

## The features that matter most

### Online booking

Clients should be able to book themselves from a link you share on WhatsApp, Instagram and Google. Check that clients can choose the service and stylist, that the system prevents double bookings, and that you can still add phone bookings and walk ins easily.

### Deposits and payments that suit Ghana

Mobile Money is how many clients prefer to pay. Look for software that accepts MoMo deposits at booking and records MoMo, cash, card and bank transfer at checkout. Deposits paid in advance are one of the strongest defences against no shows.

### Reminders on WhatsApp

Your clients live on WhatsApp. Automatic reminders before appointments, and follow ups after a missed one, should go out on WhatsApp or SMS without anyone on your team having to send them by hand.

### Staff commission and payroll

The system should record which stylist did each service and work out commission for you, for any pay period. This saves hours each month and builds trust with your team.

### Clients and loyalty

Look for client profiles with visit history, total spent, birthdays and notes, plus a simple loyalty programme that rewards your regulars.

### Stock and expenses

If you sell retail products, you need stock tracking with low stock alerts. Recording expenses such as rent, wages and supplies lets you see real profit, not just takings.

## Practical questions for Ghanaian salons

### Is it priced in cedis?

Prices in dollars change with the exchange rate, which makes budgeting difficult. Look for clear cedi pricing, and check what is included on each plan, especially staff limits and the number of branches.

### Does it work well on a phone?

Owners, managers and stylists will often use the system on their phones between clients. Test it on an ordinary Android phone, not just a laptop.

### Can it grow with you?

If you plan to open another branch, check how multi location works and what it costs. If you train apprentices, see whether the software can track them.

### How is data stored and protected?

Ask where data is stored, whether it is encrypted, and who can see it. Your client list is one of your most valuable assets.

### What support is available?

When something is not working on a busy Saturday, you want quick help from people who understand how salons in Ghana run.

### Can you try it before paying?

A free trial lets you test the real daily tasks: setting up services and staff, taking a booking, checking out a client and running a commission report.

## Questions to ask any provider

- Can clients pay a deposit by Mobile Money when they book?
- Can reminders be sent on WhatsApp?
- How is stylist commission calculated?
- How many staff and branches does each plan include?
- Can I import my existing client list?
- What happens to my data if I stop using the software?
- How do I get help, and how quickly?

## Planning the switch

Move over in stages. Start by adding your services, staff and client list, and take new bookings in the system. Once that feels normal, run all checkouts through it, then switch on reminders and commission reports. Train one or two people well first, so they can help the rest of the team.
