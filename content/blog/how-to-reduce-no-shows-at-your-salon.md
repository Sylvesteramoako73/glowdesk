The most reliable way to reduce no shows at your salon is to combine three things: take a small deposit when clients book, send a reminder the day before and again a few hours before the appointment, and have a simple, written cancellation policy that you apply fairly to everyone. Each of these helps on its own, but together they change how clients treat their booking, from a loose plan to a real commitment.

## Why no shows hurt more than they seem to

A missed appointment is not just one lost service. The chair stood empty for the whole slot, the stylist was paid or at least present, and another client who wanted that time may have been turned away. For services that take hours, such as braids, locs or a full set of extensions, a single no show can wipe out most of a stylist's day.

No shows also affect your team. Stylists on commission lose income they were counting on, and the uncertainty makes it harder to plan staffing for busy days like Fridays and Saturdays.

## Step 1: Take a deposit when clients book

A deposit is the single most effective tool against no shows. When a client has paid something upfront, they are far more likely to turn up, or at least to tell you early if they cannot.

To make deposits work:

- **Keep it reasonable.** A deposit is a commitment, not the full price. Many salons ask for a fixed amount or a portion of the service price, especially for long services.
- **Make it easy to pay.** Most clients in Ghana prefer Mobile Money. Accept MoMo as well as cards so nobody has an excuse.
- **Explain what happens to it.** Tell clients clearly that the deposit comes off their final bill, and what happens if they cancel late or do not show up.
- **Use it for long or high value services first.** If you are unsure, start with services that block the most chair time.

## Step 2: Send reminders that people actually read

Many no shows are simply forgotten appointments. A short, friendly reminder fixes a surprising number of them.

A good rhythm for most salons is:

- **The day before:** a reminder with the date, time, service and stylist, and a simple way to reschedule.
- **A few hours before:** a short nudge on the day, especially for morning appointments.

WhatsApp is where most of your clients already are, so reminders there are more likely to be seen than email. Keep the message warm and personal. Use the client's name and the stylist's name, and avoid long blocks of text.

## Step 3: Make rescheduling easy

Clients who cannot make it often stay silent because cancelling feels awkward or difficult. Make it easy to change a booking:

- Put your WhatsApp number or booking link in every reminder.
- Ask clients to let you know if they cannot come, rather than just to "confirm".
- Thank people who reschedule early. You want to reward the behaviour you want to see.

An early reschedule is good news. It gives you time to offer the slot to someone else.

## Step 4: Write a clear booking policy

A short policy, shared before clients book, sets expectations and makes it easier for your team to enforce them politely. It should cover:

- How much notice clients must give to cancel or reschedule
- What happens to a deposit after a late cancellation or no show
- How late a client can arrive before the appointment has to be shortened or moved

Put the policy on your booking page, in your WhatsApp business profile and in your booking confirmation. Apply it fairly. If you make exceptions for some clients and not others, the policy quickly stops working.

## Step 5: Follow up after a no show

How you respond to a no show matters. A calm, friendly message the same day often recovers the client:

- Let them know you missed them and hope everything is fine.
- Offer a new time.
- Remind them of your policy, gently, if a deposit was involved.

Track repeat no shows. If a client misses several times, it is reasonable to ask for a full prepayment before you book them again.

## Step 6: Fill gaps when they happen

Even with deposits and reminders, some clients will still miss. Have a plan to fill the gap:

- Keep a short waiting list of clients who want earlier appointments.
- Post a quick "slot available today" message on your WhatsApp status.
- Use the time for walk ins, training or product restocking.

## Step 7: Measure your no show rate

You cannot improve what you do not measure. Each month, count how many appointments were booked and how many were no shows. Look at patterns:

- Are certain services, days or times worse than others?
- Are new clients more likely to miss than regulars?
- Did no shows fall after you introduced deposits or reminders?

Even a rough monthly figure shows whether your changes are working.
